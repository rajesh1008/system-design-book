fig-20-1: A diagram in which a caller's question and signed grant go to the agent loop, which asks the itinerary graph who is connected and asks the semantic layer of 41 metrics for a metric, the layer hands compiled SQL to the query guard, the guard runs it against a read-only warehouse and returns rows, and the guard writes every decision to the trail.
fig-20-2: A class diagram of semantic layer version 1.0, which holds 41 metrics such as refunds_count, each naming one table, one aggregate kind, an owner, a window of 400 days, and the roles that may ask, and each grouping by up to three ordinary dimensions or, only if granted, by restricted dimensions such as customer_id for risk and approver_id for human resources.

fig-20-4: A two-column table matching kinds of questions to what answers them: how many or how much and trends over weeks go to the semantic layer, which bookings share a flight and how two records connect go to the graph, a one-off join goes to an analyst, and whether this person may change it goes to the grant, which neither store holds.
fig-20-5: A flowchart in which a query and its grant pass five gates in order, restrict to the layer, estimate bytes, dollars, and seconds, check limits on window, a one-dollar cap, and sixty seconds, check the entitlement by role, desk, and column, and run read-only with at most 1,000 rows, with each gate able to send the query to a refused box and every outcome written to the trail.
fig-20-6: A histogram of estimated over actual bytes for 332 answered queries larger than 10 megabytes, clustered just below one with a median of 0.94, and a marker at 0.91 for the year scan, showing that the estimates run slightly low.
fig-20-7: A flow in which the question how many refunds last year becomes raw SQL that joins on a JSON field and filters with LIKE, which estimates at 13 months, 44.0 terabytes, and $220.12 and is refused on cost, while the same question through the layer becomes refunds_count for calendar 2025 and returns 704,000 for 10 megabytes and $0.0001.
