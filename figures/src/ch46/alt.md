fig-46-1: A table of the seven levels of the design interview, one row each from level one to level seven, showing what the level adds (one agent and one tool, acting on its own, retrieval and memory, durability, the control plane, security and evaluation, fifty use cases), the gap it leads with, and the rubric bar out of fourteen, which rises from eight to twelve.
fig-46-2: A timeline of forty-five minutes: the top band is the clock, with marks at five minutes to frame and estimate, fifteen to draw the chain, thirty to close the five gaps, and forty-five to cost and defend; the lower band is the answer from the opening, with a red hatched block from minute three to minute thirty-one labeled the model and the chain drawn only at minute thirty-one.
fig-46-3: A top-to-bottom diagram of level one: a customer's request reaches the refund agent, whose only effect is a typed proposal; a bold arrow takes the proposal to an amber gate for permission up to $1,500, which the finance owner grants from outside the loop; the gate writes to the ledger, and a separate verifier reads the ledger.
fig-46-4: A top-to-bottom diagram of the level-two fare watch: an hourly trigger over 1,840 watches starts the loop, a bold arrow takes each voucher to an amber gate holding $100 a voucher and $5,000 a day, the gate calls voucher_issue, a verifier with its own identity reads the result and reports to the owner on the loop, and the owner holds a kill switch that stops the loop.
fig-46-5: A top-to-bottom diagram of level three: fare rules stored by version and a memory with an owner and an expiry both feed the agent; a bold arrow leads to a proposal that cites the version it read and then to a gate; a dashed arrow sends a memory write to the memory owner, who signs it into the memory.
fig-46-6: A sequence diagram of level four: the worker writes step four and its key to the durable log, calls the rebook tool with that key, and crashes, shown in red; the resumed worker reads step four and the key back from the log, calls the tool again with the same key, receives the first result instead of a second rebooking, and marks the step done.
fig-46-7: A left-to-right diagram of level five: the refunds agent, the rebooking agent, and the fare watch all send their effects to one gateway with fourteen tools; an amber control plane holding policy, budget, and the kill switch feeds the gateway; a bold arrow runs from the gateway to the tools, and a dotted arrow writes every call to one trail.
fig-46-8: A two-column diagram of level six: on the left a customer email is read by a reader with no tools that can only emit a typed request checked by an amber policy gate, while a red arrow shows a hidden instruction in the email dropped; on the right a model update must pass a green golden-set gate of 640 tasks scored pass^k before a bold arrow takes it to traffic.
fig-46-9: A top-to-bottom diagram of level seven: fifty use cases arrive as manifests and must pass an amber conformance gate, K1 to K6, before a bold arrow takes them onto the shared platform; a grey review board with decision rights governs the gate and charges $2,000 a use case; the platform writes the result per use case to a blue evidence store that reports back to the board.
fig-46-10: A table of the rubric's seven criteria with what earns the full two points for each: framing, the effect and owner by minute five; estimation, a number from a source; chain, a gate before the effect; gaps, all five closed, highlighted green as the row every level requires; trade-offs, two options and why not; cost, per verified outcome; communication, the marks met and a record.
fig-46-11: A chart of rubric totals out of fourteen for twenty-four answers grouped by level one to level seven, with a grey bar line at each level rising from eight to twelve; green circles for the twelve worked answers all sit at thirteen or fourteen, above their bars, and red squares for the other twelve sit lower, two of them at or above the bar but still below the level.
fig-46-12: A ladder drawn bottom to top with seven rungs, level one with one tool and gap two, level two acting alone, level three retrieval, level four durability, level five the control plane, level six security, and level seven fifty use cases; a bold path climbs the rungs to a green card reading twelve worked, seven levels, one rubric; dashed notes for the clock at five, fifteen, thirty, and forty-five minutes and for the rubric of seven criteria point at the middle rungs.
