# ch12 alt text (one line per figure; copied into 03-figures/alt.md for the build)
fig-12-1: Table of sixteen failure modes grouped by plane. Action has seven, from a model call that times out to a partial effect across two suppliers; control has three; knowledge has two; trail has three; one row, outlined in red, sits outside every plane: an effect path outside the gateway, an unknown kind of failure seen only by a credential list.
fig-12-2: Table of retry policies for nine operations across four planes, giving timeout and tries. Reads and policy decisions retry because they are reads; effects with a native key or a lookup and trail writes retry under the ledger key; an effect with no key and an approval wait are marked in red as not retried.
fig-12-8: Diagram of two paths to the payment processor. The agent loop's refunds pass the policy engine and the gateway, which holds the key issuer, ledger, trail, and the gw-effects credential. The involuntary-refund batch for CXL-0414 gets the same permissions but calls the processor through a framework adapter with an 8-second timeout, three tries, and a new key per attempt, under the int-fin-02 account and with no ledger entry. The processor talks to a slow card network and sends a settlement file the next morning.
fig-12-3: Two sequence diagrams. With a key per attempt, the caller sends a refund with key fw-1, hears nothing in 8 seconds, sends again with key fw-2, and receives two credits for one refund owed. With a key per action, both sends carry the same key, the processor replays the first result, and one credit is paid.
fig-12-7: Two stacked charts for April 14, 2026, 2:55 to 4:05 p.m. CDT. The top shows slow processor calls rising from zero to about 65 percent just after 3:08 p.m., holding until about 3:44 p.m., and falling to zero by 3:50 p.m. The bottom shows duplicate credits each minute, between one and twelve, from 3:10 to 3:47 p.m., totaling 212 and $61,000. A dashed line marks the alert at 3:52 p.m., after the last duplicate.
fig-12-4: Flow diagram of an effect call under a key. A yes sets the ledger to committed, a no sets failed, and no answer before the timeout sets unknown, which a lookup or replay under the same key resolves to committed. Committed and failed entries go to verifiers per leg, the PNR, voucher, and settlement-file checks, which find duplicates, missing effects, or one leg of two done.
fig-12-5: State diagram of the circuit breaker. Closed allows effects. It opens when 40 percent of calls in 20 seconds, with at least six calls, are slow or failed. Open holds effects and moves to half-open after 15 seconds. Half-open sends read probes only; two probes under 2 seconds close it, and a slow probe reopens it.
fig-12-9: Sequence diagram. The processor sends the reconciler the April 14 settlement file with 5,358 credits. The reconciler takes the 5,146 refunds owed, groups credits by charge, and reports to the risk team 212 extra credits worth $61,000.00 and zero refunds owed and not paid.
fig-12-6: Ladder of four service levels from top to bottom. Full service proposes, permits, acts, and verifies. A breaker that is open, or kill switch tier 1 or 2, drops it to propose-only, where permissions are granted and effects wait with their keys. A control plane that is down or stale facts drop it to answer-only. Kill switch tier 3 drops it to people-only.
fig-12-10: Two charts. Left, new duplicate refunds by day in April 2026: zero every day except 212 on April 14, with a dashed line at April 28 marking the fix. Right, the verified-outcome rate at three checkpoints: 97.9 percent in December, 98.8 percent in March, and 98.1 percent in April.
