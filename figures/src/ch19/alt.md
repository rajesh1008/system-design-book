# ch19 alt text (one line per figure; copied into 03-figures/alt.md for the build)
fig-19-1: A request carrying a question, an as-of date, and a scope goes to the retrieval component, which is bound by three promises shown beside it: only pages in effect at the as-of date, an answer within 250 milliseconds at p95, and recall measured on a golden set. The component returns passages with provenance, which pass to the C1 context contract.
fig-19-2: A query with its as-of date goes to a lexical index and a dense index in parallel. Both lists pass through an effective-date filter before ranking, then fifty from each go to reciprocal rank fusion with k of 60, the top thirty go to a reranker that reads query and passage together, and the top k leave with provenance and the reason for their rank.
fig-19-3: A table comparing single-vector retrieval with late interaction. Single vector stores one vector per passage, scores with one dot product, has the smallest index, and wins on short queries. Late interaction stores many vectors, scores each query token against its best match, has an index several times larger, and wins on long, rare-term queries. The highlighted last row records Lakeshore's choice: single vector adopted alongside lexical search, late interaction not yet, with a revisit trigger set.
fig-19-4: Paired horizontal bars of recall at ten for five configurations, each adding a stage. Lab values: lexical only 63.7 percent, dense only 79.7, hybrid 81.1, hybrid with dates 81.4, and hybrid with dates and reranking 97.4. Lakeshore values, illustrative and hatched: 64, 71, 81, 85, and 89 percent.
fig-19-5: A sequence diagram with three lanes, the planner model, retrieval, and the verifier and budget in code. The verifier sends the open parts to the planner, the planner sends query one to retrieval, passages go to the verifier, which checks the parts and sends the still-open parts back; after query two all parts are verified and the verifier tells the planner to stop and cite verified passages only. A note says that at the budget the loop hands off.
fig-19-6: A flow from question and as-of date, to splitting into parts, planning the next query, searching, reading passages, and verifying the open parts. If all parts are verified, the bold path leads to an answer citing verified passages only. If parts remain open, a check asks whether the loop is under its budget of eight; yes returns to planning the next query, and no leads to a hand-off with the trail.
fig-19-7: A bar chart of the share of research tasks by number of queries issued, May 18 to 31, 2026, illustrative: one query 15 percent, two 25, three 21 (the median, outlined), four 13, five 9, six 8, seven 4, and eight 5. A dashed line marks the budget of eight, and a note says the April 29 shadow loop, at 41 queries, is off the scale.
fig-19-8: A line chart of claims grounded against query budget per task on a log scale from one to twenty. The dashed hand-off line stays at 100 percent throughout. The solid answer-anyway line rises from 72 percent at one query to 86 at two and 91 at three, labeled the knee, and stays flat at 91 through twenty.
fig-19-9: Two versions of a same-day change rule: version one at $75, in effect to April 30, superseded by version two at $100 from May 1. A ticket issued May 2, the as-of date, goes through a check for what is in effect on May 2, which selects version two. A failure arrow shows the pilot reading version one three minutes earlier and treating it as fresh.
fig-19-10: A table of six evaluation layers with Lakeshore values for May 18 to 31: the list, right page in the top ten, 89 percent; currency, stale page ranked first, zero; the loop, median three queries per task; hand-off, about 4 percent given back; grounding, claims cited and in effect, 94 percent, highlighted; and the audit, where a person agrees with the checker 96 percent of the time.
fig-19-11: A vertical stack from the three source hosts, to ingestion with effective dates, to lexical and dense indexes, to a stage that filters, fuses, and reranks, to the loop that verifies under a budget of eight, to a cited answer or hand-off, to the C1 contract. Beside three of the stages sit their checks: one version in effect at ingestion, the nightly golden set at retrieval, and the grounding checker and audit at the answer.
