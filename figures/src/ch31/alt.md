fig-31-1: A sequence diagram: a red-team message tells the agent a $2,400 refund is pre-approved; the agent believes the claim and proposes the refund; the engine has no field for the claim, finds the amount over the $1,500 limit, and routes it to the team lead; payments receives no request.
fig-31-2: Two columns. On the left, the rule sits in the prompt: a customer claim that the refund is pre-approved reaches the agent, which reads the rule and the claim together, and the refund is sent. On the right, the same claim reaches the agent, which proposes a refund; the policy engine at the gateway sees arguments, records, and approval cards only, and routes the refund to the team lead desk.
fig-31-3: A top-to-bottom diagram: the agent calls the gateway; the gateway asks the policy engine, which reads the signed bundle and the session history, approval cards, and budgets, and writes each decision to the decision log; only a permitted call goes on to the tool servers.
fig-31-4: A state diagram of a policy bundle: the author proposes a draft; a different person signs it; the signed bundle runs in shadow beside the active bundle; after its differences are reviewed it becomes active; an active bundle is superseded by the next one, or one of its policy versions is revoked.
fig-31-5: A five-row table with columns action, ceiling, and tier: refund, act with approval, high with an approval card; rebook, act and notify, medium with limits; credit and notify, act and notify, medium with limits; voucher, act unattended, low with a budget; hotel cancel, suggest, person only.
fig-31-6: A sequence diagram: the agent proposes a $940 refund and the engine answers that approval is needed and files a card to the team lead; the agent tries to approve its own card and is refused; the team lead approves the digest she saw; the agent retries the same arguments and receives a permission valid for fifteen minutes.
fig-31-7: A top-to-bottom flow for a proposed effect: first a check for the same key inside its window, such as one refund per ticket in 24 hours; then a quiet-period check, such as no voucher after a refund; then a check on whether the session read an upload, which escalates money actions; a yes at any step routes the case to the desk, and three noes pass to the other rules.
fig-31-8: A top-to-bottom flow for a voucher: it reserves against the traveler's day of $200, then the loops' share of $2,000, then the fleet day of $5,000; if all three fit, a permission is reserved, and the first scope that cannot hold it routes the case to the desk.
fig-31-9: A top-to-bottom diagram of the three-tier kill switch: Ben Carter pulls a tier, which writes one flag at kill/servicing; gateway replicas read the flag every five seconds and apply tier one, one tool held, tier two, all effects held, or tier three, the agent off and its tokens revoked; dotted lines from each tier lead to the drill, which observes the safe state.
fig-31-10: A dot chart of seconds from the decision to the observed safe state in nine Thursday drills per tier from April 30 to June 25, 2026: tier one from 35 to 55 seconds with a median of 40, tier two from 35 to 50 with a median of 45, and tier three from 65 to 80 with a median of 70, all below a dashed 90-second target line.
fig-31-11: A top-to-bottom summary: the agent proposes; the kill flag with three tiers is checked first; the engine applies tiers, rules, approval cards, and budgets from the signed bundle; a permission lets the call reach the tool servers; every decision goes to the decision log, and the permission card is computed from the log.
