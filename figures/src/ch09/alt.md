# ch09 alt text (one line per figure; copied into 03-figures/alt.md for the build)
fig-09-1: A killed worker writes to the run store's checkpoint and journal, a restarted worker resumes from them, a sweeper reads the stored timers and escalates to the approver desk, and the desk's grant reaches the restarted worker before it makes keyed calls to the airline and processor.
fig-09-2: A checkpoint holding position, step outputs and schema, written in the same commit as the step journal, and owning a pending approval request, a permission reference and idempotency keys, with durable timers stored beside it.
fig-09-3: A sequence in which the engine journals the start and result of the model's flight choice and of the rebooking, and then a replay that reads the recorded outputs and calls neither the model nor the airline.
fig-09-4: A replay loop that reads each journaled step, uses the recorded output in strict replay or a fixture in a what-if replay, compares the decision fields, and stops at the first step that differs.
fig-09-5: A table of five options against what survives a restart, from a durable execution engine and Lakeshore's March run store, which keep everything, to the February checkpointer and the pilot loop, which lose the pending approval and timers.
fig-09-6: The states of a week-long wait, from filed to waiting, with reminders at 24 and 72 hours and an escalation on day 5 returning to waiting, and endings at granted or expired on day 7.
fig-09-7: A sequence in which process A files a request and stores escalation and deadline timers before it is killed, and process B finds the escalation due, marks it fired, escalates, receives the grant, and cancels the deadline.
fig-09-8: Line chart of the share of kill points that resumed correctly at each of the twelve steps: the run store stays at 100 percent, the February checkpointer drops at steps 4 and 6 through 10, and the pilot loop fails from step 4 on.
fig-09-9: Bar chart of Ben Carter's replay benchmark on 2,000 trajectories: median 0.61 seconds, 90th percentile 1.3, 99th percentile 2.4 and slowest 2.9 seconds, all under the three-second target line.
fig-09-10: The durability scorecard with four measures: resume rate after a kill 100 percent, replay 99th percentile 2.4 seconds against a target under three, waits outstanding without a timer zero, and approvals re-filed by a restart zero.
