# ch42 alt text (one line per figure; copied into 03-figures/alt.md for the build)
fig-42-1: A top-to-bottom flow of the review as a design instrument: a submission file goes through the five gap questions and a sixteen-item checklist by plane to a verdict of approve, change, or decline; approve and change write a signed decision record with a revisit into the record store, whose revisit queue reopens the submission when a date or trigger arrives; decline sends the team back to draw a new design.
fig-42-2: A stacked bar chart of review outcomes per month from November 2025 to September 2026: one change in November, a change and a decline in January, one change in May, five changes and one approval in June, one approval each in July and August, and a decline and a change in September, fourteen reviews in all.
fig-42-3: A class diagram of the decision record: a review writes exactly one record holding context, a one-sentence decision, consequences, a revisit date and trigger, an owner, and a signer; the record contains two or more options, one chosen and each loser with its reason, and one or more evidence links; the record store holds every record and answers which are due and which were revisited.
fig-42-4: A table of the production checklist by plane, split into shape items whose failure declines a design and value items whose failure changes it: action has the gateway and a stop condition as shape items, and a retry with the same key and a pinned model as value items; control has permission outside the loop as its shape item, and limits in policy, a tier-one hold, and a signed decision key as value items; knowledge has no shape items, and source, age, and owner as value items; trail has a verifier that did not act and reads its own source as shape items, and the counted result and the record as value items.
fig-42-5: A table of the five gap questions, one row per gap from G1 to G5: what is proposed, who permits it, whether a retry is the same request and what stops the run, who verifies, and which number shows the result; points transfer v1 answered only the first, and v2 answered the first four, with the business-result row still open.
fig-42-6: A left-to-right diagram of the declined points-transfer design: the member asks the points assistant, which the gateway permits under the August bundle; the assistant debits the points ledger and calls the partner's transfer API with a new reference on each try, then polls the partner's status with no stop condition and treats its own read of that status as verification, both marked as failures.
fig-42-7: A left-to-right diagram of the points-transfer design after review: the loyalty desk approves, the gateway permits under the transfers-v1 rule and logs the permit, the points assistant proposes once with the permit id as its key and stops, the ledger transfer service posts to the partner, and a transfer reconciler that did not act matches the partner's daily statement to the permission log and sends anything unposted at 72 hours back to the desk.
fig-42-8: A table of the review card filed on September 8, 2026: fourteen designs reviewed, two declined, nine changed, three approved as submitted, zero shipped without a kill switch in a highlighted row, and fourteen records with three revisited.
