fig-43-1: A diagram of the method applied six times: six worked designs feed two columns of steps, frame the effect, estimate, and draw the chain on the left, and close five gaps, cost it, and record the decision on the right; both columns end in a double-bordered green box for the designs linter, which leads to a card reading six designs, six records, thirty gaps closed.
fig-43-2: A plane map of the telecom care agent: the account holder or line user talks to the agent harness in the action plane, which reads accounts, plans, and the outage record from the knowledge plane and sends a proposal to the policy engine in the control plane, scoped by line and amount, with a care desk promised in 30 minutes; a permission goes to the care executor, which writes billing and provisioning; two reconcilers in the trail read those records and feed the status the customer sees.
fig-43-3: A table of the telecom design's five gaps, each row green as closed: G1, a typed credit or plan proposal, owned by the care architect; G2, line scope and amount, owned by privacy counsel; G3, a key and an order id, owned by the care architect; G4, two reconcilers, owned by billing operations; G5, cost per contact, owned by the head of care.
fig-43-4: A diagram of Lakeshore's autonomy table in the worked design: the agent harness sends every proposal to the policy engine, which routes vouchers up to $100 to act alone, rebookings up to $250, or $400 for weather, to act and notify, refunds up to $1,500 to approval, and hotel cancellations to suggestion only; refusals and approvals pass through desks promised in 30, 60, and 240 minutes and ordered by departure time, and every effect ends at three double-bordered reconcilers.
fig-43-5: A plane map of the bank payments agent: accounts, payees, and transactions in the knowledge plane feed the agent harness; its proposals go to a policy engine that reads network rules and the Regulation E clock in the control plane, with a disputes desk beside it; permissions go to the payment executor, which writes core banking and the network gateway; a new payee is refused in red and sent back to the customer's app; two reconcilers in the trail read the posting and case files.
fig-43-6: A table of card-network rules held in the permission plane: Visa 12.6 for a duplicate, filed by day 120; Visa 13.6 for a credit not processed, from day 15 to day 120; Mastercard 4834 for a duplicate, by day 90; Mastercard 4853 for a credit not processed, checked against the credit reference; and, in a blue row, Regulation E error claims, 10 or 45 days.
fig-43-7: A plane map of the insurer claims agent: the policyholder or claimant talks to the agent harness, a deterministic waterfall computes the amount, the policy engine in the control plane grants or sends the claim to an adjuster desk for amounts over $2,500, denials, fraud, and payee changes; the claims executor pays only the payee of record through the payments bank, and claims and payment reconcilers in the trail read what the bank and claims system wrote.
fig-43-8: A plane map of the lender collections agent: the borrower talks to the agent harness, which proposes to a policy engine that checks contact count, hour, medium, protected flags, and the plan matrix; protected cases go to a hardship team promised in four hours; the collections executor writes loan servicing and the message platform, whose delivery log feeds the contact count back to the policy engine; contact and payment reconcilers sit in the trail.
fig-43-9: A sequence diagram of the collections human gate: a borrower's reply says lost my job; the agent proposes a payment plan, flagged; the policy engine refuses it in amber and sends a case summary to the hardship team with a four-hour promise; a person clears the flag; the reconciler confirms to the policy engine that nothing was sent after the flag.
fig-43-10: A grid of nine patterns against six designs, telecom, rebooking, refunds, bank, claims, and collections: six patterns in bold above a dashed line are filled in every design, permission outside the loop, independent verifier, status from the chain, promised hand-off, rules outside the prompt, and tier by amount; below the line, a person decides and an effect never taken are hatched in every design except rebooking, and a signed surge rule is hatched only for telecom, rebooking, and refunds.
fig-43-11: A horizontal bar chart of cost per verified outcome across the six designs, each labeled with the person cost and months to pay back: telecom $1.16 against $7.40, 2.9 months; rebooking, hatched as Lakeshore, $1.36 against $54.90, 0.7 months; bank $1.80 against $11.20, 3.9 months; collections $2.12 against $14.50, 3.4 months; refunds, hatched, $2.54 against $54.90, 1.3 months; claims $9.92 against $68.00, 7.5 months.
fig-43-12: The signature diagram of six industries and one evidence chain, top to bottom: output, proposal, a double-bordered amber permission annotated six policies, six person gates, execution, a double-bordered green verified outcome annotated with its sources, ledger, booking record, settlement, network case, claims journal, and delivery log, and business result.
